# A stalled broker-to-host stream is visible, ends within seconds, and a prompt that reached OMP is not sent twice

## Problem

A long Chat session (a 44.6 MB session file) stopped showing replies. OMP received the prompts and answered them, but the stream from the detached broker to the extension host stalled for about two minutes. The host gave up on two prompts after its 30 s command deadline (`DEFAULT_COMMAND_TIMEOUT_MS`) and reported them as unconfirmed ("may not have been delivered"); the user sent them again. Then the broker closed the consumer with `the consumer fell behind the retained lines and must reattach` (`RpcSubscriber.takeNext`, ring of 2048 lines / 4 MiB), the host reattached, and the answers appeared. The host log had no line about the stall, the close or the reattach, and the same close was found in the logs of four brokers before. Nothing in the log could say what had held the stream up.

## Goals and Non-goals

- Goals:
  1. Every close by the broker and every reattach is in the host log with its reason, the last sequence number, the lines replayed and the duration; a link that delivers nothing while the child is alive is logged within seconds with its measurements and is shown to the page as a reconnecting notice that clears when frames resume.
  2. A prompt whose answer did not arrive is looked up in the session file before it is called unconfirmed; found, it is delivered and the transcript is shown from the file until the stream recovers, so the user does not send it again.
  3. A consumer that cannot drain its socket is closed by the broker after about 10 s, with its own reason, instead of waiting for the ring to evict its cursor; the check of pending rows saved on another branch reads the session file, not the whole transcript, for a large session. (The user also asked for a rebuild from the file after such a close and after `unknown_since`. Review showed that a reattach already reads only the entries after its cursor, and that `unknown_since` is the one case where the file is known to disagree with the process, so neither was changed; see Current State.)
     Amended 2026-10-29: when the broker's ring no longer holds the lines after the window's cursor, the reattach does rebuild the transcript from the file; see "Amendment 2026-10-29".
  4. Per-frame processing time and bytes per second are measured in the frame pumps on both sides, so the next occurrence names its trigger.
- Non-goals: changing the ring size or the pace bound; changing the command deadline; changing what a frame is; a new transport; making a large `get_entries` smaller on OMP's side.

## Current State

- `RpcLineRing` keeps 2048 lines / 4 MiB; each consumer has one cursor (`RpcSubscriber`) that sends replay and live lines in order and stops at `RPC_PACE_BYTES` (2 MiB) of unflushed socket data, resuming on the socket's `drain` event. A cursor that falls behind eviction is closed with the fell-behind reason. A consumer paced for a long time was left alone until the ring evicted its cursor, minutes later on a quiet session.
- The host's watchdog (`#lastActivityAt`) fires on 90 s of silence; any frame, including the frames of a large answer, resets it.
- A command deadline passes a send to the page as `unconfirmed`; `#lostPromptCheck` looks only at the stream.
- A reattach and a reload read `get_entries { since: cursor }` after painting the file's tail, so they ask for the entries after the file's last one, not the transcript. A full `get_entries` (tens of MiB of `rpc_chunk` lines for the incident's file, through the cursor that carries prompt responses) comes from `unknown_since` (the process refused the cursor), from a deferred full read, and from `#dropPendingSavedElsewhere`, which runs when a ring replay leaves pending rows in a session with branches.
- The host logs nothing when the broker closes the connection (`RpcHandle.closeChannel` only publishes the event), nothing when the reattach starts or finishes, and no traffic figures.

## Cause

**Not proven.** Two candidates fit what was logged, and the new measurement is meant to settle which:

1. The host's own full `get_entries` (`unknown_since`, or `#dropPendingSavedElsewhere`) queued a chunk train of tens of MiB in the single per-consumer cursor ahead of the prompt responses. Frames flow, so the watchdog never fires, the responses wait behind the train, the 30 s deadline passes, and a later burst evicts the cursor from the ring. This is consistent with the log (responses missing while OMP answered, the close only minutes later) and with a 44.6 MB file. It is inferred from the code and the symptom, not observed.
2. A write-queue stall in the broker's socket (`writableLength` staying high although the kernel took the bytes, or a missed `drain`). The logged `fell behind` close can only be raised by `takeNext`, which the pump reaches only while the queue is below the pace bound, and every ring line pumps every consumer, so a pump that "never resumes" does not fit the incident as logged; this candidate survives only as a queue that stays high, or as a host that was not reading, which is candidate 1's consumer side. The measurement separates them: the host's busy time and unread bytes against the broker's unflushed bytes at the close, and `missedDrains` (the queue found empty with no drain reported since the pace began, counted apart from a queue merely partly flushed).

A test reproduced neither: a real broker with a stuck reader closes the consumer as paced (see Verification), which shows that the stall is bounded now, not which cause it had.

## Proposed Design


### Broker (`src/broker/rpc-ring.ts`, `src/broker/pty-broker.ts`)

- `RpcSubscriber` records when a pump stopped at the pace bound (`pacedAt`) and clears it when a pump goes below the bound. `enforcePace(limitMs)` is called by a timer while any consumer is paced and returns `"closed"` (it stood at the bound for `limitMs`: the sink is closed with `RPC_PACED_REASON` and the kind `paced`), `"resume"` (its queue is below the bound again: the broker pumps it through `pumpConnection`, so the exit barrier runs and transient lines are released) or `"wait"`. The timer runs only while someone is paced; its period is a quarter of the limit, between 50 ms and 1 s.
- The limit is `RPC_PACED_CLOSE_MS` = 10 s, taken from `--rpc-paced-close-ms` (200 ms to 10 min), which is a test seam: production always uses 10 s. It is a broker launch argument like the other `--rpc-*` options; an older broker has no such option and keeps its behaviour.
- The other forced close, a cursor evicted from the ring, keeps its reason and now has the kind `fell-behind`.
- `RpcSink.close(reason, forced)` records `lastForcedClose = { reason, atMs }` in the broker. The next `rpc-attached` answer carries `lastForcedClose: { reason: "fell-behind" | "paced", ageMs }` when the broker has closed a consumer, so the host that reattaches knows why it was closed even though the wire carries no reason: the transport ends the connection and the handle sees only "the peer closed the connection".
- Measurement: `RpcSubscriber.stats` counts lines, bytes, pumps, busy time, the slowest pump, `pacedEpisodes`, `resumedBelowBound` (the timer found the queue partly flushed) and `missedDrains` (the timer found it empty with no drain reported since the pace began; the broker's `onDrain` calls `noteDrain`). The broker logs one summary line per consumer at close (`rpc consumer summary: …`) and one line per pace episode once it has lasted half the limit (`rpc consumer paced N ms: …`). Every pump is timed with `performance.now()`.

### Wire contract (amends ADR-0038 §1; no protocol version bump)

- `rpc-attached.lastForcedClose?: { reason: "fell-behind" | "paced"; ageMs: number }` is an additive optional field. `parsePtyFrame` ignores a missing field, a reason it does not know and a non-numeric age (the attach still works). An older broker sends none; a newer broker with an older host is harmless: the older parser copies only the fields it knows. The field is diagnostic: nothing acts on it.
- `PtyLaunchSpec.rpcPacedCloseMs` becomes `--rpc-paced-close-ms` on the broker's command line. It is not part of the broker's identity, so a running older broker is not replaced.

### Host transport (`src/wire/pty-ipc.ts`, `src/host/rpc-handle.ts`, `src/host/broker-handle.ts`, `src/protocol/rpc.ts`)

- Each connection keeps a read-side meter (`RpcChannelTraffic`): frames, characters, reads, busy time (parse plus listeners), the slowest read and frame, the wall-clock time of the last frame, and the socket's `bytesRead`, `readableLength` and pending write bytes. `RpcChannel.traffic()` returns a snapshot.
- `RpcChannel.probeLink(timeoutMs)` is one round trip of the broker's own `status` request (the same request `refreshStatus` sends): the milliseconds it took, or `null` when it was not answered in time or the connection is closed.
- A `closed` event carries the transport's detail text and `byClient: true` when this window ended the connection on purpose (`BrokerHandle.disconnect`; the flag is cleared when a connection is listened to again). `attach` returns `lastForcedClose` from the frame.
- One `RpcForcedCloseReport` and one `RpcChannelTraffic` type, declared in `protocol/rpc.ts`, serve the broker, the transport, the handle and the fake.

### Host session (`src/host/rpc/session.ts`)

1. **Visibility.** At Info: `the broker connection closed: <detail>; last seq N, M lines since the attach; <traffic>` (with ` (this window replaced it)` when `byClient`), `reattach started: last seq N`, `reattached: replay from seq … (the broker holds … to …; …); the broker (any window) closed a consumer as paced (its socket did not drain) 2 s earlier`, `reattach finished in N ms after K attempts: last seq A to B, L lines received, phase live`, `reattach failed …`. The traffic line gives frames, reads, bytes from the socket, characters decoded, bytes unread, busy time, the slowest read and frame, the age of the last frame, bytes unsent.
2. **Stall monitor.** A 1 s timer (armed with the watchdog) compares the time since the last line with the age of the oldest pending command (a slash prompt, a command with its own timeout and any wait while a dialog is open are exempt; `sentAt` restarts when a dialog settles). It takes a traffic reading only when it has something to log or every 5 s. Three cases, none of which replaces a connection: (a) a command has waited 5 s and lines still arrive: one Info line per command, `the answer to X has not come for N ms, but frames are still arriving` (candidate 1); (b) a command has waited 5 s and no line arrives: one Info line per stall with the link's traffic, then one **link probe** per stall (`probeLink`, the broker's `status` request, which OMP never sees and which travels the same broker-to-host queue as the lines). An answer logs `the broker answered a status request in N ms: the link carries traffic, so the stall is OMP holding the command` and the page is not told; no answer within the probe deadline (15 s) logs a warning (`the broker or the link is stalled`) and tells the page; an answer still outstanding after 2 s tells the page until it arrives. A starved command ends its stall when any line arrives, and `frames resumed after N ms without one` clears the notice. (c) OMP is working and sends nothing for 5 s with no dialog open: one Info line per silence, no probe and no notice, because a long tool is normal. `link_stalled` is a host-owned frame (`ChatEventFrame`), carried in the snapshot head only while set, shown in the banner of a live conversation (it replaces a read-only reason's text while it lasts, which is intended; a failure outranks it). The banner says the Chat reconnects on its own; the worst case is the broker's paced close within 10 s when data is queued behind the host, otherwise the watchdog (45 s of silence while working, 90 s idle, plus its 15 s probe).
3. **A prompt whose answer did not come.** When a `prompt` send times out as `unconfirmed`, the host refreshes its history reader and looks for a user message with the same text and a timestamp at or after the send (less 1 s; one predicate, `isSendRow`, serves this and the lost-prompt check). Found, the send resolves as `accepted` and the entry is not marked unconfirmed; the entry is marked only when the file does not hold the message, so the lost-prompt check cannot take it for lost while the file is being read, and an entry that left the ledger meanwhile stays reported lost. While the link is stalled and the window is live the transcript is applied from the file and re-read every 2 s so the reply shows without the stream, and lines resuming stop that (during a reattach nothing is applied: the delta read that follows brings the row, and an older read must not move the cursor back; legacy-id files are not applied). Not found, the send stays `unconfirmed` as before. OMP writes the user message before it answers, so a message on disk was delivered whatever the link did to its response. The match is by text and time because a user message entry in the files read here holds only role, content and timestamp, no request id. `steer` and `follow_up` sends have no ledger entry and are not covered: OMP writes them when it delivers them. A message of the same text sent twice within a second can be matched by the first copy. `HistoryReader` serialises every public read (`refresh`, `snapshot`, `loadOlder`, `recentOffPath`) on its own queue, because a `refresh` can re-index under a read that computed its positions before an await; `HistoryPort` requires `indexedBytes` and `recentOffPath`.
4. **Large sessions.** `#dropPendingSavedElsewhere` (the check that pending rows were saved on another branch) no longer sends a full `get_entries` for a session file of 8 MiB or more (`largeSessionBytes`; a full answer is 8 MiB of chunk lines, four times the pace bound and twice the ring): after a fresh `refresh` (a re-index in progress reports no size) it asks the history reader for the newest 400 chat entries off the active path (`recentOffPath`) and logs once that it did. ADR-0051's rule that the disk serves only when it agrees with the live cursor is about the disk *window*; this check runs once the session is settled, on rows of a branch that was persisted before the user moved away, so it needs no §4 amendment. A reattach, with or without a close by the broker, is unchanged: it reads only the entries after its cursor. `unknown_since` stays with the process, the authority on the cursor it refused; deferred full reads are unchanged.
5. **Measurement at Debug.** While frames arrive the monitor logs one `traffic: …` line every 5 s at Debug (the cumulative counters above, so a rate is the difference of two lines); the Info lines above carry the same counters at the moments that matter.

### Page and docs

`linkStalled` joins the snapshot head and the banner. The troubleshooting guide lists the new log lines and what each means; the changelog has one Fixed entry; `docs/architecture.md` (ring pacing, watchdog), ADR-0038 (banner) and ADR-0050 (note) record the contracts.

## Alternatives

- **Split prompt responses from the bulk lane.** A second cursor for responses removes candidate 1 at its root but changes the ring's contract (ADR-0038 §1 and the design's retention rules) and every consumer; not justified before the measurement says which candidate happened. Serving the saved-elsewhere check from the file removes one producer of bulk lines from the same cursor; `unknown_since` and deferred full reads remain.
- **Raise the ring or the pace bound.** Delays the fall-behind and keeps the stall; the symptom is a missing reply for minutes, not an eviction.
- **Close on `RPC_PACE_BYTES` at once.** A slow but live consumer would be dropped on every large replay; the 10 s stand at the bound is the point where a drain would have come.
- **Probe the link with a new command, or with OMP's `get_state`.** OMP answers `get_state` from its serial command queue, so it measures OMP, not the link, and its answer ends the very stall it measures. The link probe is the broker's existing `status` request: no new frame, and OMP never sees it.
- **Match the prompt in the file by request id.** The entry carries none in the files read here. Text and time are what the file has.
- **Reload on a stall without a probe.** A transient hold-up of a second or two would reset a working connection.

## Risks and Open Questions

- **Cause open** (see Cause). The host's busy time and unread bytes, the broker's unflushed bytes and `missedDrains`, the broker status answer and the `frames are still arriving` line separate the candidates in the next log.
- A host event loop blocked for more than 10 s (another extension, a debugger pause) now makes the broker close the consumer; the close is lossless while the ring retains the lines and the reattach is cheap.
- `lastForcedClose` is broker-wide and not cleared: a reattach of another window within the age shown can report a close that was not its own. Attributing it per window needs the attach frame to carry a frontend identity, a second wire change for a diagnostic line; instead the log line says `the broker (any window) closed a consumer` with the age, and nothing acts on it.
- **Decision: an unanswered broker `status` probe never replaces the connection by itself.** A broker-to-host queue that stays at the pacing bound is closed by the broker within 10 s, before the probe's 15 s deadline. Every other silent link is replaced by the watchdog (45 s working, 90 s idle, plus its probe). A probe deadline that passes right after the host's event loop was blocked fires before the socket is read, so it would report a healthy link dead and fail its pending commands as unconfirmed. A faster replacement needs evidence from the new log lines of a stall that neither the paced close nor the watchdog ends; it should then replace only when, after one more I/O turn, `socketBytesRead` has not moved since the probe was sent.
- The link probe is made once per stall: an `ok` verdict is reused if a different command starves later in the same stall, and a `failed` verdict caused by a host event-loop block keeps the notice until a line arrives.
- A message sent twice with the same text inside one second can be matched by the first copy: the second is reported delivered although only one reached the file; a prompt sent twice in a second with identical text is the user's own duplicate.
- Reading a 44 MB file in the host for `recentOffPath` / `snapshot` costs an indexed refresh, not a full parse; the history reader already indexes by line offsets. The off-path read is bounded at 400 entries.
- `linkStalled` is a new snapshot field: an older page ignores it, an older host sends none. Both parsers tolerate the other.
- A broker that is older than this build keeps its old close behaviour (no paced close, no `lastForcedClose`); the host-side visibility and the file-served paths work with it.

## Rollout and Verification

One change: broker (ring, options, attach field), host transport meter and link probe, session behaviour, protocol and page banner, docs. Unit tests: ring (paced limit, restart on drain, resume and missed-drain counting, kind of a fell-behind close, pump statistics); broker argument bounds; `rpc-attached` field parsing; the transport meter; the history reader's `indexedBytes`, `recentOffPath` and queue; the session with a fake channel and clock (close and reattach lines and the typed by-client flag, the starved-command log, the link probe answered, unanswered and absent, the quiet-session line, a dialog, link-stalled banner on and off, file-confirmed prompt, later reply followed from the file, no full read after a paced close, one process full read after `unknown_since`); chat model, parser and banner. A real-broker test (`src/host/rpc-handle.test.ts`; it needs the built `out/pty/pty-broker.js`): a consumer that stops reading is closed as paced after the shortened limit, the next attach reports `lastForcedClose.reason = "paced"`, the reattached window receives the lines after its cursor, and `probeLink` answers from the broker and returns `null` on a closed connection. The host-side recovery from that close is the fake-channel session test: the real-broker test stops at the handle because the stand-in child does not speak the chat protocol. The full unit suite runs at Idle; the VSIX is built and installed only through `pack-commit.ps1`.

## Amendment 2026-10-29: a reattach always ends, and a gap in the ring is read from the file

### Incident

One window held two Chat tabs that both streamed heavily (one had read about 240 MB from its broker since it attached). The extension host stayed blocked for tens of seconds at a time, twice for about a minute and a half; the log named no Desk operation. The broker closed both consumers as paced (their sockets did not drain while the host did not run), each tab logged `reattach started` and then **nothing**: no attach, no failure, no retry. The tab showed "Connecting to the session…" for 44 minutes until the user reloaded the window. After the reload the log showed the broker's ring had moved on (`replay from seq 0 … the lines in between are gone`).

### Cause (proved by failing tests, not by observing the incident)

`connectPtyBroker` (`src/wire/pty-ipc.ts`) races its first awaits (connect, then the hello) against `failure`, a promise that the 10 s handshake deadline, a socket error and a socket close reject. After the hello was written it awaited `answer.promise` alone. A deadline or a close at that point rejected `failure`, which nobody observed any more, so the dial **never settled**. A blocked event loop makes exactly that happen: Node runs the due timers before it reads sockets, so the 10 s deadline fires, destroys the socket and rejects `failure` while the broker's answer waits unread in the kernel buffer. Everything above the dial then waited on it:

- `RpcHandle.reconnect()` is one shared flight (`reconnecting ??= …`); a dial that never settles pins it for good, so every later attach joined the same dead flight.
- `RpcSession.#reattach` held `#reattaching` and `#establishing`; the phase stayed `attaching` (the banner "Connecting to the session…"), the watchdog was not armed (it arms only when `live`), and **Reconnect** was refused as already started.
- Nothing was logged, because the only lines of the path are written when an attempt ends, and `catch {}` around the channel's attach dropped the reason of a failure.

### Decisions

1. **Every wait on the path has a bound, and an attempt that exceeds it ends in a retry or in the visible failure.** The handshake's answer is raced against the same `failure` as its earlier steps, and a failed dial destroys its socket. A deadline that fires more than 250 ms late proves this process did not run (the broker was not silent), so the dial gets one fresh 10 s bound and says so in its error. The dial of `RpcHandle.reconnect()` is bounded to 30 s and a connection that arrives after that is closed (the status round trip that follows keeps the handle's 15 s request bound). `RpcSession` cuts one attach request at 45 s (`attachTimeoutMs`; the rest of an attempt keeps its own deadlines) and ends the attempts of a reattach after 120 s in all (`reattachBudgetMs`, checked when an attempt ends), after which the ordinary failed state, its automatic slower retries and the page's **Reconnect** apply, as before. The existing attempt list (250 ms … 5 s) is unchanged.
2. **Every outcome is in the log.** Each failed attempt logs `reattach attempt N failed after X ms (<cause>); trying again in D ms`; the final failure logs `reattach failed after X ms and N attempts: <code> (<cause>)`; a first attach that fails logs `the first attach failed: <code> (<cause>)`. The cause is the log's only: a page never receives it.
3. **A gap in the ring is read from the file.** When a reattach finds that the broker's ring no longer holds the lines after the window's cursor (`truncated` in the attach answer), the window re-reads its transcript from the session file (`#rebuildFromDisk`) and asks OMP only for what the file trails by. The earlier scope decision (goal 3: no rebuild after a paced close) stays for a close whose lines the ring still holds, where the cursor's `get_entries { since }` is small. With a gap the delta is unbounded: OMP's answer would queue through the consumer the broker has just closed as too slow and be parsed in one turn on the thread that was too slow. The window logs `the broker's lines in between are gone: the transcript was re-read from the session file (last entry …)`, or, if the file could not serve it, says so and asks OMP. The page shows no notice: the transcript is whole again, and the only visible effect is the repaint.
4. **Per-frame work is attributed.** The event loop monitor (`src/wire/loop-lag.ts`) adds `meter`: work too frequent to mark one by one (a read of the broker socket, a coalesced reply update, the delivery of an output to the page, a publish of the whole transcript, the apply of a session-file snapshot, a full transcript rebuild) adds its duration to a per-name total, and the next late timer reports `metered Desk work in that time: broker socket read 5 times, 380 ms in all, longest 120 ms; …`. A stall with none of it still says that no Desk work was running. The totals are inclusive (a read contains the delivery it caused), so they are read per name, not summed.

### What was not changed

The watchdog, the ring size, the pacing bound and the command deadline are as before. The 33 ms coalescing of `message_update` still flushes before every other line, so tool-output frames interleaved with a long reply defeat it; the cost per frame is measured by the new meter first, and no pacing change was made without that evidence. Identity and re-adoption rules are untouched: a reattach still re-proves the broker's identity and the session's, and the new bounds only end attempts earlier.

## Amendment 2026-10-30: a reattach under a heavy stream is shown live and read-only until OMP confirms the session

### Incident

One Chat tab whose OMP wrote a very heavy stream (over a gigabyte read through the socket since the attach, 250–700 lines and 7–20 MB a minute) lost its connection once (the broker closed the consumer as fallen behind its retained lines) and then could not recover for about forty minutes. Every reattach succeeded (`reattached: replay from seq …`, lines kept arriving on the new connection) and failed exactly 30 s later with `state-failed`; the automatic retry and a manual Reconnect did the same. The one success came when a flood of some 42 000 lines had drained and the window's cursor was far behind the ring (the gap path).

### Cause

Not a host matching or replay defect. In each failed attempt the consumer was caught up (its cursor equalled the ring's newest seq when it closed; every line sent had been processed) and the `get_state` response never reached it. Measured against a real OMP 18.13.3 whose model streams random text (each `message_update` carries the whole partial message, so the output is quadratic; lines over 1 MiB are split into `rpc_chunk` frames): a `get_state` sent during the stream was answered 18–21 s late, with the answer queued behind 426 lines (172 MB) that OMP had already queued, and later reads stayed unanswered for more than 30 s. OMP's RPC input is handled in order and its output is written through one spool, so a response leaves only after everything queued before it, and a process that produces faster than it writes starves the answer. That is OMP's behaviour and is not changed here.

On the host, `RpcSession.#send` armed the fixed 30 s command deadline for `get_state` (exempt only from the dialog pause), and `#resync` turned a read that timed out into `SessionFailure("state-failed")`. `#reattach` ended in the failed state, the automatic retry attached again and queued a new `get_state` behind the same backlog, which is longer than before, so the loop never ended. The test `session-reattach-stream.test.ts` reproduces it with the fake channel and a manual clock: 100 s of streamed lines after a reattach, no answer, phase `failed`.

### Decisions

1. **The reads of a reattach wait while the link delivers.** A *patient* command (`get_state`, `set_ask_dialog`, `set_subagent_subscription`, `get_subagents` of the `#resync` that follows a **reattach**) restarts its deadline from the newest line (`#lastActivityAt`) when it fires: it fails only after the link has been silent for the command deadline, or at `starvedReadMs` (default 5 min) after it was sent, whatever arrives. A quiet link, a wedged dispatcher that says nothing and an answer that never comes with lines still arriving all end as before, in `state-failed` with `nativeStateUnanswered` (the evidence the page's Restart needs). The deadline of every other command, the watchdog probe included, is unchanged, and so is the resync of a first attach and the heal resync of a frame that could not be folded. **This supersedes the 2026-10-29 "120 s in all" bound for one attempt:** `reattachBudgetMs` now bounds the re-attempts (it is checked when an attempt ends and no new attempt starts after it), and a single attempt may last up to `starvedReadMs` while its state read waits.
2. **A reattach shows what it has while it waits.** After the attach and the paint, `#stateOrStalled` watches the read: if it is still out after `resyncLiveAfterMs` (default 2 s) and lines arrived within that time, the session reads a gap in the ring from the session file (`#readRingGap`, local I/O only), publishes the conversation `live` with a read-only reason (`STATE_UNCONFIRMED_REASON`, "Waiting for OMP to confirm this session; sending is paused until it does.") and keeps waiting for the answer. Nothing that needs the session proven is accepted meanwhile: `#identityVerified` is reset by every reattach and set only by the answer, `#controlRefusal` answers `not-live` to sends, Stop, navigation, settings and the tool, cost and context reads, `respondUi` writes no dialog answer, `HostToolCalls.open()` is called only after the proof so a tool call that arrives meanwhile is decided after it, host tools are not registered, and the watchdog is not armed. A start (first attach) and a reattach whose link is quiet keep the old behaviour: there the page shows "Connecting" and the quiet link fails at the command deadline.
3. **The answer decides.** The unchanged tail of the resync runs when it arrives: continuation, the identity check (`identity-diverged` for another session, from the live phase as for any later check), registrations, the agent roster, `get_entries` after the cursor (the gap was already read and is not read twice), then the read-only reason is lifted and the watchdog armed. The log says `OMP has not answered get_state for N ms, but lines keep arriving (…): the tab is shown live and read-only until it does` and later `OMP answered get_state N ms after it was asked: the session is confirmed`; the extension's `chat connection restored` line waits for the state that lifts the reason.
4. **A closed link ends the wait at once.** The broker connection closing while the tab is shown this way fails the pending reads as closed (not unconfirmed, so no unanswered-state evidence is recorded), sets the phase back to `attaching` and retries the attach on the reattach schedule instead of waiting for the read's deadline and failing with `state-failed`.
5. **No second resync starts under a running one.** `#scheduleResync` (the heal of a frame that could not be folded) is skipped while a resync runs; the one in flight rebuilds the transcript.

### What was not changed

OMP, the broker, the ring, the pacing bound, the command deadline and the watchdog are as before. The page, the protocol and the wire are unchanged: the state is an ordinary `live` with `readOnlyReason`. The identity rule is as stated in ADR-0038 and ADR-0050: a reconnect cannot drive a session its attach has not proved; this amendment moves what the tab shows before the proof, not what it does.

### Risks and open questions

- A tab shown live and read-only cannot be stopped or written to until OMP answers. The same is true of the old "Connecting" state, and a command sent now would queue behind the same backlog; the page shows the reason in the composer and the banner. If OMP stays starved, the read ends at the cap (5 min) in `state-failed` with Restart.
- `starvedReadMs` and `resyncLiveAfterMs` are fixed defaults (options of `RpcSession`, not settings); 5 min is a bound on how long a never-answered read may keep a tab waiting while lines arrive, chosen well above the 18–21 s measured and well below the forty minutes of the incident.
- The starvation itself (OMP answering behind its own output) is not removed; a heavy stream still delays every command it is asked, Stop included, by the backlog.
