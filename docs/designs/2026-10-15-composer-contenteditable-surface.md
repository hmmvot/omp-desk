# A contenteditable editing surface for the Chat composer

Supersedes, in [Code blocks in the Chat composer](2026-10-15-composer-code-blocks.md): the Goals bullet "Native Undo/Redo", the `execCommand`-and-`blockEditRef` mechanism of its section 2, section 3 (Rendering), Alternatives 1 and 2, the Risks "Prose changes font", "Re-wrap at a fence" and "A line that ends in blanks…", the band and hit-test items of Rollout step 3, and its Smoke section. It supersedes, in [Paseo-style chat](2026-10-04-paseo-style-chat.md), the *Composer* paragraph ("the editor stays the native `<textarea>` … mirror layer") and the native-undo mechanism of *Single source of truth and Undo*. Everything else in the code-blocks design (the fence grammar, the keys inside a block, the hint, the completion gate, the indentation rule, what is sent) stays in force. The decision record is [ADR-0077](../decisions/0077-edit-the-chat-draft-in-a-contenteditable-surface-rendered-from-the-markdown-string.md).

**The maintainer's decision (2026-10-15):** in the Chat composer only the fenced block is monospace (the editor font, on a band); prose stays proportional. This replaces the open "maintainer reading" of the code-blocks design, whose whole-draft-monospace compromise is rejected.

## Problem

The shipped composer is a `<textarea>` with a transparent-text highlight layer behind it. A textarea has one font, so while a draft holds a fenced block **the whole draft** switches to the editor font and a band marks the block. The maintainer wants only the block monospace. A textarea cannot do that without desynchronising caret, selection and hit tests from the painted glyphs (the code-blocks design's Alternative 1).

The code-blocks design rejected a `contenteditable` surface (its Alternative 2) for three reasons; this design answers each:

1. *Re-rendering discards the browser's undo history.* True and measured (see Undo): the page therefore owns undo and redo.
2. *Everything built on `selectionStart`/`setSelectionRange`/`caretRowEdges` would be rewritten.* Accepted as the cost. The members the composer uses are mirrored on one surface object, with a retained selection like a textarea's, so the call sites keep their shape; `caret-row.ts` is replaced by a rect-based measurement.
3. *Paste and IME differ between engines.* Paste is always plain text and takes `text/plain` only; IME composition is left entirely to the browser and read back when it ends (see IME). The engine is the one VS Code ships (Electron's Chromium); `engines.vscode` is `^1.110.0` and the installed 1.142 has `beforeinput` with `getTargetRanges()`, `Intl.Segmenter` and `caretPositionFromPoint`.

A prototype (one `<div>` per line, prose in the UI font, block lines in the editor font with a band, own undo stack) passed 55 scripted checks in an owned Chromium: typing, the fence keys, selection across prose and block, every model offset round-tripping through the DOM, native-undo behaviour, image chips, paste, drop, completions, auto-grow, and an emulated IME composition. Its report also named what it could not show: a real Windows IME, assistive technology, and the cost of porting every textarea-bound path of the real composer.

## Goals and Non-goals

- Goals:
  - Only the lines of a fenced block use the editor font and the band; prose keeps the proportional UI font, with the same metrics (padding, 20 px line height, two to eight rows, scroll) as today.
  - Every composer feature keeps working through the existing `Composer` API (Chat callers do not change): Enter/Shift+Enter/Alt+Enter/Ctrl+Enter, the block keys, slash, argument and `@` completions, image markers, paste, drag and drop, the draft hand-off across an editor replacement, queued-message restore, Rewind prefill, `set_editor_text`, Add Selection to Session, prompt history, focus rules (held focus, type-to-focus, `ompDesk.focusComposer`).
  - The plain Markdown string stays the single source of truth: what is stored, handed off, restored and sent is that string, byte for byte as before.
  - Undo and Redo keep working from the keyboard, including over image markers and block edits.
  - IME composition is never interrupted by the page.
  - No textarea remains in the composer.
- Non-goals:
  - Rich text, syntax highlighting while typing, line numbers.
  - Native features that exist only for form controls: spell-check suggestions (already off), in-editor drag-move of selected text, the Edit menu's Undo/Redo items (see Platform facts).
  - The AskEditor (the `input`/`editor` dialog field) stays a textarea: it has no blocks and no markers.

## Platform facts (VS Code webviews, verified on the installed build 1.142)

These constrain the design and were checked in `out/vs/workbench/contrib/webview/browser/pre/index.html` and the workbench's webview command handlers, not assumed:

- The webview script's `keydown` handler calls `preventDefault()` on `Ctrl+Z` and `Ctrl+Y` and, in Electron, on `Ctrl+C`/`Ctrl+V`/`Ctrl+X` and `Shift+Insert`, and then forwards the key to the workbench. The workbench registers handlers for the Undo, Redo, Select All, Copy, Paste and Cut commands which post `execCommand(...)` back to the frame, run as `contentDocument.execCommand(name)`. So in the product **a keyboard undo reaches the page as a `keydown` and is followed by an `execCommand('undo')`**, never as a `historyUndo` the page can rely on; Cut and Copy arrive as `cut`/`copy` events (no `beforeinput`), Paste as a `paste` event.
- The script also cancels the default of every `drop` at the content window. A text drop therefore inserts nothing unless the page's own element-level `drop` handler (which runs first) does it.
- A file drag over the page is blocked by the workbench's own overlay unless `Shift` is held; the Composer's document-level handlers (unchanged) accept the drop that gets through.

Consequences adopted below: undo and redo are driven by the keyboard, and `historyUndo`/`historyRedo` `beforeinput` events are cancelled and ignored; the workbench's `execCommand('undo')` after a handled `Ctrl+Z` fires no `beforeinput` at all, so what keeps it from undoing a second time is that **no live node the browser edited is left for its own undo stack to point at** (every native edit is read back and the touched line elements are replaced, see IME and section 2); the `cut` handler must itself delete; **Edit > Undo and Edit > Redo are inert for the composer** (certain, not a risk); the end-to-end suite must prove the shortcut set in a real window, because the owned-Chromium path (editing commands sent through the DevTools protocol) is not the product's path.

## Current State

- `Composer.tsx` reads and writes the draft through `value`, `selectionStart`, `selectionEnd`, `setSelectionRange`, `focus` and `document.execCommand('insertText'|'delete')` at about 48 places; the browser's own undo stack covers every edit that goes through `execCommand`.
- `DraftMirror` paints image-marker chips and the block bands behind transparent text, kept equal to the textarea's metrics by `syncMirror`; `caret-row.ts` measures visual rows with a hidden clone of the textarea for the history arrow keys; `autosize` measures `scrollHeight` with a held parent height.
- `use-held-focus.ts`, `type-to-focus.ts`, `focus-trace.ts` (its `disabled` attribute filter) and `src/shared/selection-query.ts` (reads a textarea's value) address the field as a `textarea`.
- A textarea keeps its own selection while it is unfocused, and `focus()` restores it; the draft hand-off freezes it with `readOnly`, which also makes IME composition impossible (ADR-0050 requires the capture to freeze every content mutation).
- Tests: `chat-ui.test.ts` has 235 `textarea` references (plus setup through `document.execCommand('insertText')`), `queued-messages-ui.test.ts` 6, `theme-ui.test.ts` 4 lines (16 occurrences), `todo-agents-ui.test.ts` and `frame-focus-ui.test.ts` a few; the `focus-trace` and `type-to-focus` unit tests; and the end-to-end suite finds the composer as `getByRole("combobox", {name: "Message OMP"})` in `test/e2e/support/desk.ts` (used by 13 scenario and readme specs), `05`, `35`, `36`, `readme-screenshots.spec.ts` (`[role=combobox]`) and `test/e2e/tools/mcp-probe.mjs`, and reads `toHaveValue` in `36`.

## Proposed Design

### 1. Surface: one `<div role="textbox" contenteditable>` rendered from the model

The composer's input is a `div.omp-composer-input` with `contenteditable`, `role="textbox"`, `aria-multiline="true"` and `spellcheck="false"`. It has **no React children**, and **every attribute that changes while the user types is owned by one framework-free controller**, `DraftSurface` (`src/webview/lib/draft-surface.ts`), written through `configure(...)` from a layout effect of the `DraftEditor` component; React renders the element with `ref` and a class only. No React commit therefore writes `contenteditable`, `data-*` or `aria-*` on the field, and nothing reconciles its children.

The DOM is a rendering of the model, one block-level line per `\n`-separated line:

- `div.omp-line` per line; a line of a fenced block (fence lines included) also has `omp-line--code`, a fence line `omp-line--fence`. Code lines use `font-family: var(--omp-mono)`, `font-size: min(var(--omp-mono-size), 15px)`, `tab-size: 4`, the same `line-height: 20px`, and the code-block background reaching the surface's full width by a negative margin equal to its padding; the text keeps the surface's padding, so a wrapped code line breaks where the surface does. Prose lines inherit the UI font. Lines break with `overflow-wrap: break-word` (as the textarea's own stylesheet does, so a long URL or minified JSON wraps instead of overflowing). Text direction is the page's (left to right in VS Code webviews), as it was for the textarea. Forced colours paint the band as `Canvas` with a `CanvasText` border.
- The band is a property of each line and lines are adjacent, so adjacent blocks, an empty line next to a block and an unclosed block need no piece logic: `DraftMirror`, `syncMirror` and `draftPieces` are deleted.
- An empty line is `<br>`. White space is `pre-wrap` per line, so trailing and repeated blanks keep their width and a tab honours `tab-size`. A line that **ends in a chip** gets a trailing `<br>` (a rendering filler that is never part of the model and is ignored by the read-back) so the caret has a position after it. A line that is only a chip, or holds **two adjacent chips**, has no extra node: the position between the two elements is a DOM boundary (`[line, k]`) that the mapping can address, and the owned-Chromium tests state whether the caret can be placed and painted there; if it cannot, the chips are given an inline-block box with no model change. Nothing is ever added to the model for a caret position.
- An image marker (`[Image #N, WxH]` whose number belongs to an attached image) is an inline `span.omp-chip` with `contenteditable="false"`, `role="img"`, `aria-label="Image #N"` (the thumbnail's own alt text) and the literal marker text. **Which numbers are attached is an explicit input**: the surface takes a `knownImages(number)` predicate from the composer and re-renders the affected lines when `refresh()` is called, which the composer does after every image publish (attach, prune, send). The same predicate drives render, edit widening and selection snapping. A marker whose number is unknown is ordinary text.
- Height needs no measuring: `min-height` (two rows plus padding), `max-height` (eight rows plus padding) and `overflow-y: auto`.
- The placeholder is drawn by CSS from `data-placeholder` while `data-empty="true"`. The same string is the accessible name (`aria-label`), so role-and-name locators keep finding `Message OMP`. At `compositionstart` the surface sets `data-empty="false"` so the placeholder never sits under the first composed characters.

**Incremental rendering.** The controller keeps a signature (text, class, marker spans) for every rendered line. A change that only alters a line's text updates that line's text node **in place**; a line's element is replaced only when its class or chips change, and every line is replaced once after an IME composition (see IME). Between the first and the last differing signatures only the differing lines are touched, so a keystroke costs time in its own line, and a fence typed on line 3 re-renders the following lines only if their block status changes. This keeps assistive-technology "text changed" events and the Windows text-input framework's view of the surrounding text as small as the platform allows.

### 2. Model, writes, and who owns what

`DraftSurface` holds `text` (the model string) and a **retained selection** `{anchor, focus}` in model offsets, updated on every `selectionchange` inside the field and after every edit. `selectionStart`/`selectionEnd`/`selectionDirection` return the live DOM selection when it is inside the field and the retained one otherwise, exactly like a textarea; `focus()` focuses with `preventScroll` and restores the retained selection (a bare `focus()` on a contenteditable would otherwise put the caret at the start of the draft after any click elsewhere, which would break inserting at the caret from Add Selection to Session, Attach, `ompDesk.focusComposer`, the focus restore after a re-attach, Rewind and "Add to draft"). The selection is never left on the root element or after a line's trailing `<br>` (native typing there creates stray root-level nodes): `setSelection` always targets a position inside a line, and a `selectionchange` that lands on the root is normalised once the pointer is released (never during a drag, which would fight the user).

Three kinds of change:

- **User edits** never reach the DOM natively. Every non-composition `beforeinput` that is cancelable is cancelled and turned into one *edit* `{from, to, insert, selection, kind}` on the model, applied by `apply`, which (1) widens `from`/`to` over any marker they cut into, so a marker is only ever removed whole, (2) records the undo step, (3) updates the model, (4) updates or replaces the changed lines, (5) puts the selection where the edit says and scrolls the caret into view, and (6) reports the new text synchronously through `onEdit`. The composer's own keys (block `Enter`/`Tab`/`Shift+Tab`) call `apply` with the edits that `code-fence.ts` already computes. A non-cancelable non-composition `beforeinput` is **not** applied to the model: it opens the same window as a composition (no page write until its read-back) and the native edit is read back, so the text can never be inserted twice.
- **External writes** go through one function of the composer, **`writeDraft(text, { caret?, history })`**, which sets `textRef`, calls `surface.setText(text, { caret, history })` synchronously, then `setText`. The `history` argument is stated by each caller, never guessed from the text:
  - `"reset"` (clears undo and redo): send, a consumed `/rewind`, Rewind's `undo` answer, **Rewind's placement** (it replaces `images` wholesale and restarts the numbers, so the old text must not be restorable over the new images), and the hand-off restore (a restored hand-off starts a fresh stack).
  - `"record"` (one undo step): prompt-history recall, queued-message restore, `set_editor_text`, an accepted completion, a Commands-row insert. This makes those writes undoable, which a textarea's native stack did not do for a `value` replacement.
  - `replaceRange(from, to, insert)` (an undoable edit that reports through `onEdit`) replaces `execCommand` for the inserted markers, Add Selection to Session text and the thumbnail ×; removing several markers with one number is **one** step (`replaceRanges`).
  The layout effect of `DraftEditor` only seeds the surface on mount (a field remounted while the draft is frozen starts frozen); in development it asserts `surface.text === text` after each commit **when no composition window is open and no write is queued**. The invariant does not depend on React's scheduling, because every writer updates the surface synchronously. Writes queued in a window are merged in order with one rule: a `reset` is never downgraded by a later `record`; the last text wins.
- **Native edits** that the surface did not make (an IME commit, a non-cancelable `beforeinput`, `execCommand` called by the workbench or a test, an `input` event outside a composition) are re-read from the DOM into the model and re-rendered. The read tolerates a changed structure: consecutive inline nodes at the root form one line, a block element is a line, a trailing `<br>` is not a newline. A marker the read finds damaged is repaired with `repairDamagedReferences`. The line elements the read touched are **replaced, never patched in place**, so the browser's own undo stack is left pointing only at detached nodes (this is what makes the workbench's `execCommand('undo')` after a handled `Ctrl+Z` find nothing to undo).

`beforeinput` cases (cancelable, non-composition): `insertText` (data), `insertLineBreak`/`insertParagraph` (`\n`), `insertReplacementText` (dictation or an OS replacement; target range from `getTargetRanges()`, else the selection), the `delete*` family, `insertFromPaste`/`insertFromDrop` (handled by the `paste`/`drop` events, so ignored here), and `historyUndo`/`historyRedo`, which are cancelled and **ignored**: undo and redo are driven by the keyboard (see Undo). Any other type (`formatBold`, `insertFromYank`, `insertTranspose`, …) is cancelled and ignored, because a change the model did not make would desynchronise it.

Deletion uses the range the browser computed (`getTargetRanges()` on a cancelable `delete*` event covers graphemes, words and lines, and was measured present in the owned Chromium), widened over markers. When the event offers none, the surface computes the same from the model: one grapheme (`Intl.Segmenter`), one word, to the line start or end.

**Freeze.** The draft hand-off's `read` calls `surface.freeze()` synchronously and `release` calls `thaw()`. While frozen the field is `contenteditable="false"` with `tabindex="-1"` (so it keeps the focus) and `aria-readonly="true"`; every mutation entry point (`beforeinput`, `paste`, `cut`, `drop`, undo, redo, `apply`, `replaceRange`, `setText`) refuses, and no composition can start. `thaw()` restores the retained selection and recomputes `contenteditable` from "frozen or disabled", never simply to true. This is stronger than a textarea's `readOnly` on purpose: ADR-0050 requires the capture to freeze every content change, and a composition would otherwise change the captured draft or lose its text when the editor is replaced. `read` also refuses while a composition window is open or a deferred write is pending (below).

### 3. Own undo and redo stack (`draft-history.ts`, pure)

Native undo is dead once the page replaces the nodes the browser edited (measured: five inserts with a rebuild after each leave nothing to undo, and `execCommand('undo')` reports success while changing nothing). The surface keeps a stack of `{text, anchor, focus}` snapshots.

- A step is recorded before every edit. Consecutive single-character typing at a contiguous caret merges into one step while keystrokes are less than one second apart, and so do consecutive Backspace or Delete presses; a newline, a paste, a drop, a cut, a block edit, a marker insertion, a replacement, a completion, an IME commit and an undo or redo each stand alone. A new edit clears the redo stack.
- `Ctrl+Z` undoes; `Ctrl+Y` and `Ctrl+Shift+Z` redo (Cmd on macOS for symmetry; the extension is Windows-only). The page handles the `keydown` itself; `historyUndo`/`historyRedo` events are ignored (Platform facts). The selection returns to what it was before the step, direction included.
- At most 200 steps and two million retained characters, oldest dropped first; a single snapshot over the character cap is kept alone (the latest step always survives).
- Image attachments follow their markers, as before: a marker that comes back by Undo re-references its still-retained image. `"reset"` writes (above) are what keep a restorable old text from meeting a replaced image list.
- The stack belongs to the mounted surface: it does not survive the field being unmounted while a dialog owns the composer.

### 4. IME composition: one window owned by the surface

A *composition window* opens at the first `compositionstart` (or at a non-cancelable `beforeinput`, which is treated the same way) and closes only when a *settle* runs with no composition active. Korean (each syllable) and continuous Japanese typing end one composition and start the next within one keystroke, so the window can contain many `compositionstart`/`compositionend` pairs.

- **A composition is an edit.** At its start the surface calls `onCompositionStart` and the composer increments `draftRevisionRef`, exactly as `onTextChange` did for a textarea during composition. A conditional write that re-checks the revision (the clear after `send` is confirmed, Rewind's `undo` answer) is therefore skipped when the user has started composing, and text composed while a send awaits confirmation is never erased.
- **Inside the window** the surface makes no DOM, selection, scroll or attribute write (with two exceptions: `data-empty="false"` at its start, and `contenteditable="false"` when the session stops being writable, which closes the window, see below). `beforeinput` returns without `preventDefault`, so native edits go through; `keydown` handlers return at once (`isComposing`, the window flag, `keyCode 229`).
- **A write refused inside the window is deferred, never dropped.** `apply`, `replaceRange` and `replaceRanges` refuse; the composer defers every caller of them through `deferredRef` (marker insertions, inserted references, queued-message restore and Rewind already did; **the thumbnail × (`removeImage`) gets the same deferral**); a `paste`, `cut` or `drop` event arriving inside the window is cancelled and queued with its data, and applied after the settle at the then-current selection. `setText` calls are queued and merged (the last text wins, a `reset` is never downgraded); `configure` attribute writes wait for the end of the window.
- **Only an unconditional write may win over composed text**, and only after the settle: `set_editor_text` replaces the composed text, which is never reported. Conditional writes either re-check their condition when applied (the revision) or wait in `deferredRef`.
- **`compositionend`** schedules one settle with `setTimeout(0)`; a second `compositionend` before it runs keeps one task, and the settle returns at once if a composition is active (the next `compositionend` schedules it again). **The window also closes, synchronously, without waiting for `compositionend`:** on `focusout` of the field, when the field is turned non-editable (a session re-attach, a freeze), and in `dispose()`; the `DraftEditor` cleanup runs before React removes the element, so the read-back still has its DOM when a dialog replaces the field. "Close" means: settle, report the text, flush the queued writes, call `onSettled`.
- **The settle**, in this order: read the DOM back into the model; record one undo step (the model as it was when the window opened); **replace the line elements whose DOM differs from the rendering before the window, plus the lines that held the selection during it** (this normalises whatever structure the IME left and detaches the nodes the browser's own undo stack refers to, so the workbench's `execCommand('undo')` after a handled `Ctrl+Z` finds nothing to undo, without touching the rest of the draft); restore the selection; if an unconditional `setText` was queued, apply it **in place of the composed text without the composed text ever being reported**; otherwise report the composed text through `onEdit`; apply the queued attribute writes and the deferred paste, cut and drop; close the window and call `onSettled`.
- **The composer's guard is the window**: `composingRef.current` reads `surface.composing` (the window, not a React timer) and is `false` when no surface is mounted; `onSettled` flushes `deferredRef`; the draft hand-off's `read` refuses while the window is open or `deferredRef` is not empty. The AskEditor keeps its own textarea guard.
- Completions do not open or accept during the window (unchanged rule); caret and selection are not reported to React during it; a freeze cannot start inside it (`read` refuses first).

What a script cannot prove is how real Windows IMEs behave. The manual list below is kept as guidance; the maintainer decided (2026-10-15) that running it is not a release condition.

### 5. Paste, copy, cut and drop

- **Paste** is always plain text: the `paste` handler cancels the event and does **either** one thing: if the clipboard holds an image file the composer attaches it (unchanged, and the text part is dropped, as today), or it inserts `text/plain` with line endings normalised to `\n`. HTML and RTF are discarded. One paste is one undo step.
- **Copy** writes the model slice as `text/plain` only, so a selection across chips and lines yields what a textarea would, with the literal `[Image #N]` markers. **Cut** (which VS Code delivers as `execCommand('cut')`: a `cut` event and no `beforeinput`) does the same **and deletes the selection itself through `apply`, one undo step**, after cancelling the event.
- **Drop.** The document-level handlers for files and for URIs dragged from VS Code stay as they are (images attach; `Shift` while dragging is still what lets a file drag from another part of the window through the workbench's overlay). *New behaviour, to be confirmed by the maintainer:* plain text dropped on the surface is inserted at the pointer (`caretPositionFromPoint`/`caretRangeFromPoint`, falling back to the end). It is new because the webview script cancels the default of every drop, so a textarea probably inserts nothing today [INFERENCE: not measured in the installed window]. Dragging selected text *inside* the surface is disabled (`dragstart` is cancelled). The behaviour is one handler of about fifteen lines and is removed if the maintainer prefers parity.

### 6. Caret and selection mapping

The model offset of a DOM point is the sum of the lengths of the lines before it plus one `\n` each, plus the text before the point within its line, counting a chip as its marker text; the reverse walks the lines' children. The functions are pure over a root (`draft-dom.ts`), so tests and the surface share them.

- A point inside a chip maps to the matching marker offset and is snapped to the marker's edge by the existing `snapSelection` with the direction of travel and the anchor/focus order kept (`Shift+Arrow` does not reverse after a snap); a pointer selection is snapped on release, as before.
- A point at the root or at a line element maps through the child boundaries; a selection from one line to another maps to a model range that includes the `\n` between them.
- The mapping is verified exhaustively: for every offset of a mixed draft (prose, a block, chips including two adjacent chips, a chip at a line start and a line holding only a chip, a chip on a code line, empty lines, wrapped lines) model to DOM to model is the identity.
- **Visual rows** for prompt history: `ArrowUp`/`ArrowDown` ask `surface.rowEdges(offset)`, comparing the caret's client rect with the first and the last visual row of the field, with half a line of tolerance. The rects are those of the character range after the caret; at the end of a line they are those of the last character, and for an empty line the line element's own rect. An offset at a soft wrap belongs to the row of the character after it (the textarea measurement's rule). `caret-row.ts` is deleted.

### 7. Keys, completions and accessibility

- The block keys (`Enter`, `Tab`, `Shift+Tab`, `Ctrl+Enter`), the popup keys, `Escape` `Escape` Rewind, history, `Alt+Up` dequeue and the send rules stay in `Composer.onPromptKeyDown`, which now receives the native `KeyboardEvent` from the surface (the surface listens natively and calls its `onKeyDown` after its own undo/redo handling, so no React-versus-native ordering exists on the field). `Tab` outside a block moves the focus, as before.
- `completionsEnabled` still requires the caret outside a block and the composition window closed; `/` and `@` inside a block stay text, and `Tab`/`Enter` never accept a suggestion there.
- **Roles.** `role="textbox"` (the maintainer's requirement and the role that allows `aria-multiline`), `aria-multiline="true"`, `aria-label` (the placeholder string), `aria-describedby` (the code-block hint while the caret is in a block; the hint stays a `role="status"` live region), `aria-readonly` while frozen, `aria-disabled` and `contenteditable="false"` while the session cannot take a prompt (a non-editable `div` takes no focus, like a disabled textarea). `spellcheck="false"` (spell-check stays off, as the textarea had it; `insertReplacementText` is accepted only so dictation and OS replacement keep working); `autocapitalize="off"` (virtual keyboards only; `autocorrect` is Safari-only and is not set).
- **Popup.** `role="textbox"` cannot carry `aria-expanded` (ARIA 1.2), so the field has a static `aria-autocomplete="list"` and `aria-haspopup="listbox"`, `aria-controls` with the list's id **only while the list is open**, and `aria-activedescendant` with the active option; a visually hidden `role="status"` announces `N suggestions` when the list opens and when its count changes, debounced so it is not announced on every keystroke. ARIA 1.2 supports `aria-autocomplete`, `aria-activedescendant` and `aria-haspopup` on `textbox` and `aria-controls` globally; whether NVDA, JAWS and Narrator announce the pair is on the manual list.

### 8. Other readers of the field

- `use-held-focus.ts`: the held element is an `HTMLElement`; "disabled" is `aria-disabled="true"`.
- `type-to-focus.ts`: `writableComposer` finds the new class, requires `isContentEditable` and no `aria-readonly`, and puts the caret at the end by collapsing the selection to the end of the field.
- `focus-trace.ts`: the composer predicate matches the new class; its MutationObserver watches `aria-disabled` and `contenteditable` instead of `disabled`.
- `src/shared/selection-query.ts`: no change. The `shared` layer may not import webview code (ADR-0065), and its `getSelection().toString()` fallback already reads a contenteditable selection (a chip's text is its marker text; the query keeps the first non-empty line).
- `panel-actions` (`ompDesk.focusComposer`, `sendPrompt`) call the same handlers; `focusWhenPossible` takes the element and the surface's `focus()`.
- Draft hand-off: the capture is the model string and `read`/`release` freeze and thaw as above. Queue restore, Add Selection to Session (`insert-text`), Rewind and `set_editor_text` use `writeDraft` or `replaceRange`; an in-flight window defers them as today.
- Tests and tooling find the field by `role=textbox` and its name. The product exposes no textarea-shaped properties; the test **driver** (below) reads the model through the pure functions of `draft-dom.ts`.

## Alternatives

1. **Keep the textarea; the whole draft monospace while a block exists** (shipped). Rejected by the maintainer: prose must stay proportional.
2. **Transparent textarea over an overlay with per-range fonts.** Rejected (code-blocks design, Alternative 1): caret and selection come from the textarea's own single-font layout and drift from the overlay's glyphs; it also hides IME composition rendering and breaks forced colours.
3. **`contenteditable` edited natively, restyled afterwards.** Rejected: replacing the nodes the browser edited kills its undo stack (measured), and the browser's own structure (extra `<div>`s, `<br>`s, merged text nodes) would have to be normalised after every keystroke. Intercepting `beforeinput` makes the model, not the DOM, the editor.
4. **Intercept everything but keep native undo through `execCommand` writes.** Rejected: the DOM is updated from the model after an edit, which the measurement showed ends the native stack; and VS Code's own undo path (Platform facts) is a keydown plus an `execCommand`, not a `historyUndo` the page can count on.
5. **A canvas or hidden-input editor (Monaco or CodeMirror style); a rich-text library (ProseMirror, Lexical, Slate).** Rejected as disproportionate: they re-implement bidi, wrapping, accessibility and every composer interaction, add a dependency to the VSIX, and a library's document model would become a second source of truth next to the Markdown string.
6. **Store image bindings in each undo step.** Rejected: it couples the pure history module to the composer's image state. The `history: "reset"` argument of `writeDraft` achieves the guarantee without it.
7. **Make the field `readOnly`-like while frozen but keep it editable.** Rejected (ADR-0050): a composition would change the captured draft.

## Risks and Open Questions

- **IME on real Windows engines** (Japanese, Chinese Pinyin, Korean) is the largest risk and is untested by script. The maintainer decided (2026-10-15) not to make the manual list a release condition; the risk is accepted and a reported IME defect is fixed when it appears. Rollback is reverting the change: the stored and sent string is unchanged, so no migration exists.
- **Edit menu and context-menu Undo, Redo, Cut, Copy and Paste.** Edit > Undo and Redo are inert for the composer (the workbench's `execCommand` finds nothing to act on, and the page ignores `historyUndo`). Cut, Copy and Paste from the menu arrive as events and work. The keyboard shortcuts work. Verified by an end-to-end check in a real window.
- **Behaviour changes the maintainer accepted (2026-10-15):** prompt-history recall, queued-message restore, `set_editor_text`, accepted completions and Commands-row inserts become undoable steps; plain-text drop is new; in-editor drag-move of selected text, native spell-check suggestions and the Edit-menu undo are gone.
- **Lost native conveniences:** none beyond the above that the textarea provided and the composer used.
- **Assistive technology** has not been run: NVDA, JAWS and Narrator behaviour for `role=textbox` with `contenteditable`, the named chips, the hint live region, the suggestion count announcement and the `aria-controls`/`aria-activedescendant` pair is on the manual list. Forced-colours rendering of chips and band is checked only in the owned Chromium's emulation.
- **Large drafts.** Rendering is incremental by line (text updates in place); the fence scan is linear; a 10 000-line paste renders once. Undo snapshots are capped by characters.
- **Browser drift.** The design relies on `beforeinput` being cancelable for the handled types and on `getTargetRanges()` for deletions; unhandled types are cancelled, a non-cancelable one triggers a read-back, and a stray `input` re-reads the DOM, so a change in either degrades to correct-but-coarser behaviour. The real sources of an unexpected DOM change are the workbench's `execCommand`, non-cancelable input types and the tests' own `execCommand` calls (VS Code webviews run no browser extensions).
- **Caret next to a chip.** Covered by tests: two adjacent chips, a chip at a line start, a line that is only a chip, a chip at a line end, a chip on a code line.

## Manual verification: real Windows IME and assistive technology (cannot be automated)

Run in the installed extension, in a Chat editor of a real VS Code window, with **Windows' own** Microsoft Japanese, Chinese (Simplified, Pinyin) and Korean IMEs. **First run every step on the previous build (the shipped textarea composer) and record what it does; the baseline, not an assumption, defines the expected result of the new build** (for example whether Enter on the last Korean syllable commits and whether it also sends). Record results in the design's *Manual verification record* section (to be added by the maintainer) and report any difference to the author.

Places, for each IME: an empty draft; the end of a prose line; the middle of a wrapped prose line; an empty line between two prose lines; inside a fenced block (monospace band); just before and just after an `[Image #1]` chip; the first and the last row of the field.

For each IME and place:

1. Compose, convert and **commit with Enter**: compare with the baseline for "sent or not". The text is exactly what the IME committed (no duplicate, no loss), the caret ends after it.
2. Compose and cancel with `Escape`: the draft is unchanged and a running turn is **not** stopped by that `Escape`.
3. `Ctrl+Z` once after a commit: the whole commit disappears in one step; `Ctrl+Y` brings it back; the draft changes exactly once.
4. While the candidate window is open: it sits at the caret (also on a code line); `/` or `@` composed in prose opens no completion until the composition ends; no scroll jump; the placeholder is not drawn under the composed text.
5. **Continuous composition:** Korean syllables typed one after another at a line end without pausing; Japanese typed without explicit commits across several clauses: nothing sent, no text lost or reordered, no caret jump at a syllable or clause boundary.
6. Korean: `Backspace` decomposes jamo without deleting the previous character.
7. Chinese: Pinyin committed as raw Latin with Enter; `Shift` toggling Latin input in place; a candidate chosen by number and by mouse.
8. Japanese: reconversion of committed text with the conversion key, predictive candidates and clause segmentation with the arrow keys: the caret stays in the composition and no history navigation happens.
9. **Composing while the field changes under it:** while composing, make the session re-attach (or hold the draft for a replacement): the composition survives or ends cleanly and the typed text is neither lost nor duplicated.
10. Windows voice typing (`Win+H`), the emoji panel (`Win+.`), the handwriting panel, text suggestions for a physical keyboard and the touch keyboard's autocorrect: each inserts at the caret and is undone by `Ctrl+Z`.
11. After `Ctrl+Shift+L` (focus composer) the caret is where it was, not at the start of the draft.
12. Screen readers (NVDA, JAWS and Narrator): the field is an edit field named `Message OMP`, multi-line; entering a code block announces the hint; a chip is read as "Image #1"; opening a completion list announces the suggestion count and the active option.
13. Edit > Undo and Edit > Redo with the composer focused: inert and harmless (nothing is lost or doubled).

A failure to report: a message sent early, a lost, doubled or reordered character, a caret jump, an unselectable or unclickable chip, a composition that cancels itself when the status line or the queue row changes, a candidate window far from the caret, or any difference from the baseline build.

- Reviewer: independent architect (`SurfaceArchReview`, a fresh agent that had not written the document), two passes.
- Outcome: accepted after the findings were resolved in the text. First pass: accept with changes, five material findings and nine should-fix items. M1 (the undo policy of external writes was guessed from the draft's value, and Rewind could pair an old text with new images): every write now states `"record"` or `"reset"` through `writeDraft`. M2 (the read-back ran under the next composition, the composer's guard was cleared by a React timer, the held write could be undone): one composition window owned by the surface, with a settle task. M3 (VS Code's webview cancels the editing shortcuts and sends `execCommand` back; cut and drop behave differently): a Platform facts section, a keyboard-driven undo, a cut that deletes itself, Edit > Undo declared inert, and real-window end-to-end checks. M4 (a contenteditable has no retained selection): a retained `{anchor, focus}` and a `focus()` that restores it. The should-fix items covered popup ARIA, placeholder ownership, in-place text updates, non-cancelable `beforeinput`, selection normalisation, chip inputs, the test inventory and driver-first slicing, the manual checklist baseline and the precise supersession list. NM2 (the window could stay open without `compositionend`): it also closes on `focusout`, on the field turning non-editable and on dispose. NS1 to NS3 (replace, never patch, the lines a native edit touched; replace only the differing lines after a composition; defer the thumbnail × and refused paste, cut and drop) and the nits are applied.
